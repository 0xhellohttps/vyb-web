Five stadium dates produced 81% of the revenue. The other 59 shows are the operating rhythm — a theater or club night every four days, each with its own vendors, crew and settlement. The prior full year grossed $21.9M with $7.0M of profit; the current year passed that by spring.

The stadium run, per date

Seven further stadium dates are confirmed for the back half of the year, each budgeted at $12.38M: a flat $6.5M artist guarantee (52%), $2.14M production, $1.28M venue, $856K marketing, $749K personnel and travel, $535K contingency, $321K insurance. Each date is financed show-by-show at a 12% fixed return repaid from box-office proceeds within months — $1.49M of cost per date, $10.4M across the run.

Figures are the promoter's own, lightly rounded. Company and artists are deliberately unnamed. Beyond the confirmed dates sit 26 shows on sale and 100+ pending projects, including residencies and national tours, which the High case below draws on.

04 — HOW THE MONEY MOVES

One ledger. Three depths.

The promoter's systems stay where they are. At Base, VYB reads them into one event ledger. At Medium, an approval policy and the payment router move money on the promoter's own rails. At Full, agents execute inside the policy and a capital partner advances against contracted receivables, repaid from settlement.

07 — FULL

The operator, and the capital. Where the forecast becomes a business.

Full adds two things. Agents that run approvals and settlement inside the promoter's policy, which lifts routed share to 85%. And embedded finance: today each stadium date is funded at a 12% fixed return repaid within months — roughly 44% annualized. VYB, with a capital partner, advances $3.5M per date against contracted box-office receivables at 18% APR for 90 days, repaid automatically from settlement. VYB keeps a 1% origination fee and 35% of the interest.

12 — ASSUMPTIONS

Every number above traces to one of these.

01
Pricing
Implementation $25K one-time. Platform $2K / month at Base and Medium, $5K / month at Full for the agent and intelligence layer. Payments 0.40% blended on orchestrated volume. Virtual cards: VYB keeps 1% of spend as its share of interchange, on 4%–6% of paid-out volume moving to cards. All within the ranges on the VYB pitch.
02
Adoption
Routed share of paid-out volume by quarter: Medium 20% → 35% → 50% → 60%; Full 40% → 55% → 75% → 85%. Years two and three: Medium 65%, 75%; Full 85%, 90%. Artist guarantees are the last category to move.
03
Financing
$3.5M advanced per stadium date against contracted receivables for 90 days at 18% APR; VYB earns a 1% origination fee and 35% of interest, the capital partner the rest. Compared against the promoter's current 12% fixed return per date. Financed dates: none in H1, 20% of the year's dates in Q3, 30% in Q4; 80% and 90% in years two and three.
04
Finance workload
Hours of finance and admin per show today — stadium 220, arena 60, theater or club 24, small room 6 — at a $60 loaded hourly cost. Saved: 40% at Base, 70% at Medium, 85% at Full.
05
Leakage
0.25% of routed volume caught as duplicate invoices, over-budget lines or mis-paid amounts before payment. Conservative against industry reconciliation experience; counted only on volume that actually routes through VYB.
06
Volume
Non-stadium activity annualized from the trailing eight months ($18.2M paid out across 88 shows). Stadium dates at the confirmed $12.38M per-date budget. Gross at the trailing 69% paid-out-to-gross ratio. Volume held flat across the three-year view.
07
What this is not
Not an offer, a quote or a commitment by either party. Pricing, adoption and financing terms are a conversation; this page is the shape of one. Figures are the promoter's own trailing results, lightly rounded and unnamed.
