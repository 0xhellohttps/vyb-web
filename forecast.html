Why TPV is the driver

VYB monetizes the money that moves, not seats or logins. TPV here is paid-out volume — the customer's payments to artists, venues, vendors and crews — because that is what VYB orchestrates and settles. Base is the current year's shape: the operating rhythm of 88 theater, club and arena shows plus 10 stadium dates at the confirmed $12.38M per-date budget. Seven of those dates are confirmed for the back half of this year, financed show-by-show at a 12% fixed return.

Figures are the customer's own, lightly rounded; company and artists deliberately unnamed. The prior full year grossed $21.9M with $7.0M of profit; the current year passed that by spring. 26 further shows are on sale and 100+ are pending.

07 — CAPITAL EFFICIENCY

VYB's role
  • Originator. Underwrites against the ledger's settlement history; sizes the advance to production and venue deposits, never the artist guarantee.
  • Servicer. Repayment is a ledger event: box-office settlement clears the advance first, automatically.
  • Fee and participation. A 1% origination fee and 35% of interest; no funding cost, no principal at risk on VYB's balance sheet.
  • Not a lender. The capital partner is the lender of record; VYB does not use its own balance sheet in this model.

The customer's current instrument is a 12% fixed return per date on a $12.38M unit, repaid from proceeds within months. We compare the cost of the two structures for the same $3.5M over the same term rather than annualizing either, since a short-duration fixed return is not a yield.

The stadium date through each level.

The confirmed per-date budget of $12.38M. Base makes every line a tracked obligation. Medium moves it on a rail under the customer's policy. Full finances the production and venue slice — $3.42M of the $12.38M — and lets agents execute inside the caps.

The approval policy behind every payment

Set by the customer on day one and enforced by the system: recurring payees within budget and under $10,000 execute and report; $10,000–$100,000, new payees and change orders are prepared for one confirmation by the finance lead; anything over $100,000, and every talent or venue settlement, needs two signatures in order. Out-of-policy items are blocked until resolved. The rails underneath — bank wire and ACH, virtual cards with per-persona caps, a stablecoin rail for the cross-border crew presented as dollars, machine payments for data — are chosen by the router per payment; the customer sees a balance, approvals and settlement, never a network name.

15 — ASSUMPTIONS & RISKS

Every number above traces to one of these.

01
Pricing
Implementation $25K one-time. Platform $2.5K / month at Base and Medium, $5.0K / month at Full for the agent and intelligence layer. Payments 0.40% blended on orchestrated volume. Cards: VYB keeps 1% of spend as its interchange share, on 4%–6% of TPV moving to virtual cards. Within the ranges on the VYB pitch.
02
Adoption
Routed share of TPV by quarter: Medium 20% → 35% → 50% → 60%; Full 40% → 55% → 75% → 85%. Years two and three: Medium 65%, 75%; Full 85%, 90%. Artist guarantees move last.
03
Financing
$3.5M per stadium date against contracted receivables for 90 days at 18% APR, funded by a capital partner; VYB earns a 1% origination fee and 35% of interest. Financed dates: none in H1, 20% of the year's dates in Q3, 30% in Q4; 80% and 90% in years two and three. Compared against the customer's current 12% fixed return per date over the same term.
04
Gross margin
Platform 85%, implementation 60%, payments 70% (rail and network costs ≈ 0.12% of routed volume), cards 80%, financing 90% (servicing only; funding cost with the partner). Modeled, not measured.
05
Customer value
Finance hours per show today — stadium 220, arena 60, theater or club 24, small room 6 — at $60 loaded; 40% / 70% / 85% saved by level. Leakage 0.25% of routed volume.
06
Volume
Non-stadium activity annualized from the trailing eight months ($18.2M across 88 shows); stadium dates at the confirmed $12.38M per-date budget; gross at the trailing 69% paid-out-to-gross ratio. Held flat across the three-year view and the scale scenario.
07
Risks
Adoption: routed share depends on the customer moving payment categories onto VYB; artist guarantees may never move. Financing partner: the Full case needs a capital partner on acceptable terms and underwriting that the ledger must earn. Concentration: one customer is 100% of this model. Stadium calendar: financing revenue is tied to a handful of dates a year. Regulatory: routed volume at these levels carries VYB into payment-facilitator registration, with its own timeline and cost. Confidentiality: the baseline is one company's real figures, anonymized.
08
What this is not
Not an offer, a quote, a company forecast or a guarantee. Pricing, adoption and financing terms are a conversation; this page is the shape of one.
