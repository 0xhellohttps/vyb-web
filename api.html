01 — WHY AN API

Six systems. Each one holds a sixth of the answer.

Ask a room's point-of-sale whether Friday made money and it will tell you the bar did $18,742. It has never heard of the band. Ask the ticketing platform and it will tell you 743 people came; it has no idea what they were paid to see. Ask the contract and it knows the guarantee and nothing about whether it was honoured on time.

VYB sits at the point where those systems resolve into a number someone has to sign. A show on VYB carries its calendar entry, its deal terms, its POS lines, its crew and vendor state, and the settlement that closes it — as one record, in one place. That is the only reason a question like “did Friday make money, and where did it leak” has an answer at all. The API is a read over that join.

Point of sale
What the room actually sold, service period by service period. Toast, Square, Clover, Lightspeed — and read-only by construction: there is no field in VYB that can write back to your POS.
Ticketing & attendance
How many people were in the room. Every per-head figure in the system divides by this number, which is why its absence is reported rather than guessed.
Calendar
Which service period belongs to which show. Without it, a night of POS data is just a Friday.
Deal terms
The guarantee, the split, the deposit, the settlement clock — the thing every other number is eventually measured against.
Labor & crew
Who worked the night, on what basis, at what cost. The half of the P&L that never appears on a sales report.
Vendors & payouts
Every party owed money on a night, their onboarding state, and — the part that matters before a run — whether the money can legally move yet.

Settlement is where all six meet, and VYB is the seat that holds both the room's side of that dollar and the act's. Two counterparties, one reconciled record.

Payments are the rail underneath every one of these numbers, and none of that changes: the settlement record the API reads is the one VYB's own rail produces. The intelligence is what makes the record worth reading — and the record is what makes the rail worth running.

02 — WHAT IT REPLACES

Nothing. That is the whole idea.

VYB does not ask a room to move off its point of sale, its processor, its ticketing platform or its accounting stack. It sits alongside them and reads across all of them. Keep Toast. Keep Stripe. Keep the spreadsheet you actually trust.

And it can coexist for a structural reason rather than a diplomatic one: the API is read-only, and no endpoint in VYB moves money on your behalf. Something that was never given the ability to capture a payment cannot quietly turn into your processor — see the money rule.

Your POS stays your POS

Toast, Square, Clover, Lightspeed. VYB reads service periods and sales lines, and there is no field in VYB that can write back. Nothing about your menu, your pricing or your tabs changes.

Your processor stays your processor

If Stripe settles your card volume today, it still does tomorrow. VYB reads the record those payments leave behind; it does not step in front of them to answer a question about them.

Nothing to migrate

No re-platforming, no export project, no asking a box office to learn a second system. Connecting a feed is a read credential — and disconnecting it leaves your stack exactly as it was.

Replacing one would defeat the point

A tool that replaced your POS would then see only what a POS sees. The edge is the read across systems — which is precisely the thing none of those systems can do for itself.

Naming these systems is a statement of compatibility, not of partnership. They are what rooms and agencies already run; VYB is built to read alongside them.

04 — RESOURCES & SHAPE

What's in it, and what a response looks like.

JSON over HTTPS. Cursor pagination. Money in integer cents, never a float. Timestamps in UTC with an offset. And the state machines the product runs on are exposed as they are rather than flattened — a hold is one of ten bid states with legal transitions between them, not a boolean called is_held.

05 — SCOPE & PERMISSIONS

A token is a seat, not a firehose.

The permission model isn't being invented for the API. It's the one the product already runs on, enforced in the database rather than in the client: a membership carries a persona, a persona carries an allow-listed set of capabilities, and a row is visible only to a caller with a live membership on it. API tokens inherit exactly that and can only narrow it.

Narrow by default

A token is issued against a seat and a capability set. When a scope is ambiguous it resolves to less access, not more — including for the token issuer.

Your side of the dollar

The operator reads the room's economics. The act reads its own settlement against that room. Same night, two records, neither one leaking the other's.

Some things stay closed

Individual venue reviews are never attributed to their author, and a room's private operating score is not readable by the acts it books. Not everything VYB holds is something VYB will sell back.

Consequential reads are logged

Exports and money-resource reads write an append-only audit row on the account they belong to. If a token pulled your settlement history, that is on your trail, not ours.

06 — THE MONEY RULE

Reads now. Nothing in the API executes money.

v1 is read-only, and that is a rule rather than a phase.

There is no endpoint that captures a deposit, releases a payout or accepts an offer on your behalf. If it moves money, it is not in the API.

When writes arrive, they will create proposals — the same object the Concierge creates in a group chat. A proposal is a card a human taps to confirm inside the app, and the confirm tap is the record of record. Software can propose; a person executes. That rule predates the API, and the API does not get an exception to it.

None of which is a step back from payments. VYB is the payment facilitator underneath these events: it onboards the parties, accepts the tap, splits the funds and settles each side. That rail is where the economics of the company end up, and it is also where this data comes from — reconciled settlement is a byproduct of running the money, not a survey about it.

The order is just deliberate. The intelligence is what a room says yes to first; the rail is what it moves to once the intelligence has proved it sees the room better than anything else does.

07 — HONEST COVERAGE

Every response tells you what it couldn't see.

A dashboard that scores a room on partial data without saying so is worse than no dashboard, because you will act on it. So every VYB response carries a coverage block: which feeds are connected for that entity, which are partial, which are missing — and which conclusions are therefore undetectable rather than merely absent.

settlement
complete
Every date closed through VYB.
contracts
complete
Deal terms signed in-app.
pos
partial
Connected mid-window; earlier nights have no sales lines.
ticketing
absent
No attendance feed, so no per-head figure exists to report.

With no ticketing feed, a per-head figure is returned as unavailable — not as zero, and not as an estimate dressed up as a measurement. The upside of saying so plainly is that you always know exactly which feed to connect to unlock the next answer.

Coverage is scoped like everything else: it reports the completeness of your data on your entities. It is not a way to enumerate what VYB holds about anyone else.
